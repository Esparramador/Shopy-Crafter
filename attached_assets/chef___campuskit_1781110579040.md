# CampusKit Design System

## Overview

CampusKit is a collegiate, structured, and official design system purpose-built for university portals and academic management platforms. It reflects the gravitas of institutional environments while remaining usable for students, faculty, and staff alike. The maroon-and-gold palette conveys tradition and authority, while the structured layout system ensures dense academic data remains scannable and accessible.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do's

1. **Give the academic calendar prominence** -- surface upcoming deadlines, registration windows, and semester milestones on every dashboard.
2. **Display grades with clarity** -- use large, well-spaced grade indicators with clear scales (letter, percentage, GPA) and sufficient contrast.
3. **Maintain a formal, institutional tone** -- use serif headings and professional language that matches the academic environment.
4. **Follow institutional branding guidelines** -- ensure the maroon-and-gold palette is applied consistently and that university logos have proper clearance space.
5. **Support dense data tables** -- academic portals are data-heavy; use alternating row backgrounds, sticky headers, and clear column alignment.

### Don'ts

1. **Don't use casual or playful language** -- this is an official institutional tool; avoid slang, excessive exclamation marks, and informal phrasing.
2. **Don't hide critical deadlines** -- registration and assignment due dates must always be visible, never buried in sub-menus.
3. **Don't use color alone to indicate grade status** -- always pair color with text labels or icons for accessibility.
4. **Don't overcrowd navigation** -- university portals have many sections; use a well-structured sidebar with collapsible groups rather than a flat mega-menu.
5. **Don't auto-delete or auto-archive** -- academic records are sensitive; always require explicit user confirmation for destructive actions.
6. **Don't neglect print stylesheets** -- students and faculty frequently print transcripts, syllabi, and schedules; ensure clean print layouts.
